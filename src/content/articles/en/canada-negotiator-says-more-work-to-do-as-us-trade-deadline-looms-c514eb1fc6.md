- President Trump said the US-Canada deal was 'moving along' but did not confirm a final agreement. 
- Leaked details suggest the US will cut tariffs on Canadian steel and aluminium from 50% to 25%, and on automobiles from 25% to 15%. 
- Canada is considering allowing US dairy producers greater market access and removing retaliatory tariffs. 
- Economist Trevor Tombe estimates Canada could lose 90,000 jobs if the additional 50% tariffs are imposed. 
- A Leger poll found 56% of Canadians want a hardline approach in talks.

The leaked deal details suggest both sides are moving to de-escalate tariffs, but reactions from Canadian political figures show the agreement is politically sensitive. Conservative leader Pierre Poilievre warned against 'one-sided' tariffs leading to de-industrialisation, while Manitoba Premier Wab Kinew urged Canada to 'fight' rather than rush into concessions.

Provincial reactions vary. Quebec Premier Christine Fréchette is assessing the impact on dairy farmers before deciding on restoring US alcohol sales, while premiers of Nova Scotia and Yukon are ready to restore sales. Sault Ste. Marie Mayor Matthew Shoemaker said a deal with 25% tariffs on steel 'wouldn't be something to celebrate', reflecting concerns in steel-producing regions.

A leaked recording shows US Vice-President JD Vance saying Prime Minister Carney tried to 'out-tough Donald Trump' and that Canada 'climb down on a lot of issues', suggesting possible last-minute changes in the negotiations.
