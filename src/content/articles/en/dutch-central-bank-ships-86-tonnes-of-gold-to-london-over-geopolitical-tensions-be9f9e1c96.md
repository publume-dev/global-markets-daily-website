- The Dutch central bank (DNB) moved 86 tonnes of gold from North America to London, citing increasing geopolitical unrest.
- The transfer took place between March and August, with 27 tonnes physically moved from New York and Ottawa to the Netherlands, and a similar quantity and quality moved to London without melting the bars.
- After the move, DNB's gold held in New York and Ottawa fell from 31.3% and 19.7% to 18.5% each, while its gold in London increased from 18.1% to 32.1% of total reserves.
- The total Dutch gold stock was 612.4 tonnes at the end of 2025, valued at €72.2 billion.

The Dutch central bank's relocation of gold reserves from North America to London reflects a response to geopolitical risk. The specific reason is based on DNB's statement, but the choice of London over other locations may relate to its status as a major gold trading hub. The transfer did not involve melting bars, suggesting DNB may aim to maintain gold's tradability while reducing cross-border transport. However, the exact value of the moved gold has not been disclosed, and the rationale for London as the destination is not fully explained.
