- The Eurosystem will launch its Pontes service in 2026, initially charging only one-off onboarding fees.
- Pontes service hours will extend to 22.5 hours per business day, with plans for 24/7 service by mid-2028.
- The Eurosystem intends to make DLT-based tokenised securities issued via European CSDs eligible as Eurosystem collateral, a step started in March 2026.
- The Appia roadmap, published in March 2026, aims to deliver a blueprint for an integrated European tokenised financial ecosystem by 2028.
- ECB Executive Board member Isabel Schnabel argued that stablecoins cannot replace central bank reserves as the ultimate settlement asset because they cannot elastically supply liquidity in stress.

These announcements mark significant progress in the ECB's push toward tokenized finance. The Pontes service aims to provide central bank money settlement to support transactions on DLT platforms. Extending operating hours to 22.5 hours and eventually 24/7 reflects the demand for longer settlement windows in financial markets. Including DLT-issued securities as eligible collateral could enhance the liquidity of tokenized assets. Schnabel's remarks underscore the central bank's role in providing the ultimate settlement asset, suggesting concerns about stablecoins as a payment infrastructure.

These developments could influence settlement efficiency and liquidity in European financial markets, particularly for institutions adopting DLT. However, specific impacts depend on implementation details and market adoption.

Market participants will monitor the technical specifications of the Pontes service, progress on extending operating hours, and the practical steps to include DLT assets in the collateral framework.
