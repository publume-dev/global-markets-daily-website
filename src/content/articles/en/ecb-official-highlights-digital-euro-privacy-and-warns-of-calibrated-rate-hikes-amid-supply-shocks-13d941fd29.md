- The Eurosystem would not be able to identify users making or receiving digital euro payments; only banks involved could, including for AML purposes.
- The digital euro is designed to work offline, with details only available to payer and payee.
- The ECB has just launched a public consultation on new euro banknote design, indicating no plan to eliminate cash.
- In a supply-side shock, hiking rates could dampen growth already affected; policy needs to be well calibrated.
- Stagflation risk is rather remote; economy is slowing but resilient, inflation in line with June baseline.
- To strengthen European firms, remove internal Single Market restrictions to allow economies of scale.

The emphasis on digital euro privacy may assuage public surveillance concerns, but banks' role in AML highlights a privacy-compliance balance. On monetary policy, the official's caution on rate hikes during supply shocks suggests inflation pressure may not be demand-driven, implying data-dependent decisions.

Digital euro privacy design could affect public adoption and bank compliance processes, while Single Market reforms, if implemented, might boost European firms' competitiveness, though sector-specific impacts remain unclear.

Watch for results of the ECB's public consultation on euro banknote design and future monetary policy decisions reflecting calibration for supply shocks.
