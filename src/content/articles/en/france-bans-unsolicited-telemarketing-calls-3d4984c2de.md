- France's unsolicited telemarketing ban took effect Tuesday; calls are permitted only when tied to an existing contract or after the customer has previously consented to marketing contact.
- A 2025 French parliamentary report found 97% of people are annoyed by telemarketing calls, 72% are contacted on mobile phones at least once a week, and 38% are called once a day.
- Under the reform, companies must obtain written consent from customers and keep proof of that consent, according to France's Fédération de la Vente Directe.

The rule leaves two routes for a legitimate marketing call: an active contract or prior consent to marketing contact. That makes the written-consent and record-keeping requirement described by the Fédération de la Vente Directe the central compliance step for telemarketing operations.

The ban could have a cross-border employment effect. The BBC's report, citing Moroccan newspaper Le Matin, said an unnamed Moroccan government minister estimated up to 50,000 jobs could be at risk; the report gave no official statement, methodology, or timeline for the estimate.

Details left open include the legal instrument behind the ban, penalties, and enforcement mechanisms; the BBC report specified only that it took effect Tuesday.
