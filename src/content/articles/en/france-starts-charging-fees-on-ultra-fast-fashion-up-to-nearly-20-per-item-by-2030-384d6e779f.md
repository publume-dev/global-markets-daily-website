- France has started imposing fees on fast fashion items, which could reach almost €20 per garment by 2030.
- The levy came into force on Tuesday, following a law passed in June to regulate ultra-fast fashion companies such as Shein, Temu and AliExpress.
- For 2026, charges range from €0.50 on underwear to €12 for a jacket.
- The levy could reach up to €19.50 per item by 2030, with a cap of 50% of the product's pre-tax price.
- China's commerce ministry has described the French law as discriminatory and a trade barrier, saying it could violate WTO principles.

The French levy on ultra-fast fashion is structured by product category and includes a trajectory rising to 2030, capped at 50% of the pre-tax price. Since the fees apply to platforms such as Shein, Temu and AliExpress, they represent a fiscal attempt to curb ultra-fast fashion consumption.

China's commerce ministry response—characterizing the law as discriminatory and a trade barrier—indicates the measure could lead to trade friction and possible WTO involvement. However, no specific complaint or next step is mentioned yet.
