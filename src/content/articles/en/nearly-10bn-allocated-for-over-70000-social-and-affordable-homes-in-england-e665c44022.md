- UK government announced nearly £10bn initial funding for over 70,000 social and affordable homes in England over 10 years.
- Grants awarded to Greater Manchester, West Midlands, West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire, North East England, and Liverpool, with an additional £6bn previously announced for London.
- This is the first part of a wider plan to build 300,000 social and affordable homes under the £39bn social housebuilding programme.
- Of the new homes, 60% will be for social rent.
- As of March 2025, there were 1.34 million households on social housing waiting lists; earlier this month, data indicated 177,530 children were homeless in temporary accommodation.

The funding allocation comes amid significant housing demand, with 1.34 million households on social housing waiting lists as of March 2025 and 177,530 children in temporary accommodation. The government's target of 300,000 homes by 2026 appears ambitious, as BBC Verify's tracking of EPC data suggests about 203,000 new homes were delivered in England in the 12 months to June 2026, though this is an estimate. Opposition parties have reacted critically: the Conservatives called the plan "pitiful," Reform UK labeled it a "vague promise," and the Liberal Democrats urged more action. Housing charities welcomed the funding but stressed the need for sustained investment in coming years. These responses have not been independently verified.

The delivery of these homes could alleviate some housing pressure given long waiting lists and record numbers of children in temporary accommodation, but it remains significantly short of the 300,000 target. Over the long term, the focus on social rent (60% of new homes) may provide more stable tenancies and reduce reliance on temporary accommodation.

Monitor the actual construction progress across regions and local authorities, and whether additional funding is allocated to meet the 300,000-home target. Actual delivery may fall short, and opposition criticisms could affect implementation.
