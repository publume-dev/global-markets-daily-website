- The Panama Canal Authority will cut daily vessel transits from 36 to 32 starting September 15, 2026.
- The reduction is due to low rainfall caused by El Niño, with measures phased in from September 3.
- The Panama Canal is used by about 14,000 ships per year and generates about $3 billion annually for Panama.
- In 2023, the canal authority also cut vessel numbers due to drought; this year's El Niño could be one of the strongest ever recorded.

The Panama Canal is a critical trade route linking the Pacific and Atlantic Oceans. Reducing daily transits will directly impact global shipping schedules and capacity. With about 14,000 ships passing annually, the cut may lead to longer waiting times, increased transport costs, and potential delays. This follows a similar reduction in 2023 due to drought, highlighting the ongoing vulnerability of key infrastructure to climate variability.

The reduction could affect shipping companies, importers, and global supply chains, especially ahead of peak seasons. Lower transit capacity may worsen congestion and lead to route diversions or delays.

Monitor whether the canal authority adjusts transit numbers based on rainfall, and how shipping lines respond to capacity constraints. The strength and duration of El Niño will be key factors affecting canal operations.
