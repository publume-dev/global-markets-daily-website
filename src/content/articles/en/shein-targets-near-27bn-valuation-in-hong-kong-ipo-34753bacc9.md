- Shein plans to list on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange on 1 September 2026, offering nearly 280 million shares at a price range of HK$47.60 to HK$49.50 per share.
- At the top of the range, the share sale would raise $1.77 billion and value Shein at $26.8 billion, well below its $100 billion valuation in 2022.
- The IPO is backed by Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, and JPMorgan.
- Earlier attempts to list in the US and London failed due to regulatory challenges.
- Shein cited the Iran war as a factor that hit demand, increased costs, and caused delivery delays in some markets.

The price range implies a valuation far below the company's 2022 level, suggesting a more cautious assessment of its growth prospects and profitability. Shein's reference to the Iran war as a factor in demand and costs may point to geopolitical risks affecting cross-border e-commerce.

A successful listing would provide Shein with capital to support its global expansion and could boost IPO activity in Hong Kong. However, the lower valuation may raise concerns among investors about profitability in the fast-fashion sector.

Watch for the final pricing decision, and whether Shein can resolve any regulatory or operational uncertainties before the listing date.
