- N Chandrasekaran said he will not seek reappointment as Tata Group chairman when his current term ends in February 2027, saying the Tata Sons board had not reached a resolution on a five-year extension.
- One board member did not support the extension proposal when it was put before the board in February; six months later, Chandrasekaran said there had been no resolution.
- Shares of listed Tata companies plunged after the announcement, according to the report.
- Tata Trusts owns 66% of Tata Sons; the article cites board disagreements over nominations, funding approvals and a possible public listing of Tata Sons, though the group has not publicly commented on the discord.
- Chandrasekaran, 63, has been Tata Group chairman since 2017, previously led Tata Consultancy Services, and joined the group in 1987.

Chandrasekaran attributed his decision to the Tata Sons board's failure to agree an extension: the proposal was brought forward in February, one board member did not support it, and no resolution had been reached six months later. Tata Trusts holds a 66% controlling stake in Tata Sons, and the report links the friction to reported disagreements over nominations, funding approvals and a possible public listing; those disputes are attributed to reports rather than public confirmation by the group. The article does not name the opposing board member, and no successor has been announced, leaving the leadership transition unresolved.
