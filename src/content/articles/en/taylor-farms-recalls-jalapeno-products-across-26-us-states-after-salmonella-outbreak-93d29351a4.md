- Taylor Farms is recalling about 20 fresh products containing jalapeno peppers from retailers including Walmart, Target, and Whole Foods across 26 US states after a salmonella outbreak.
- The company identified a grower in Sinaloa, Mexico, as the source and said it will no longer source from that farmer.
- Coast Citrus Distributors, a supplier of the jalapeno peppers, also recalled its own fresh jalapeno because of salmonella concerns.
- Taylor Farms recently recalled iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico because of a cyclosporiasis outbreak; the CDC says more than 22,000 people have been sickened since 1 May.
- The cyclosporiasis outbreak has spread to 45 states, and Michigan health officials confirmed the first two deaths linked to it.
- Taylor Farms said it made 40% of all salad kits sold in the US last year.

Taylor Farms' estimate that it made 40% of all salad kits sold in the US last year gives the recall significant commercial weight: products are being pulled from national retailers in 26 states. The company has attributed the salmonella contamination to a Sinaloa grower and said it will stop sourcing from that farmer, while an earlier recall traced iceberg lettuce to central Mexico in the cyclosporiasis outbreak. Taken together, the two events show recurrent food-safety exposure in Taylor Farms' Mexico-sourced fresh produce, though the pathogens and products involved are different.

Public-health impact is already measurable: the cyclosporiasis outbreak has sickened more than 22,000 people across 45 states since 1 May, and Michigan has confirmed the first two deaths linked to it. For retailers, the new recall means pulling jalapeno products from shelves in 26 states in the same season as the lettuce recall.
