- UK government analysis estimates the crackdown on zero-hours contracts could cost businesses between £350m and £2.9bn per year.
- The middle estimate of the policy's overall cost is £1.1bn.
- The analysis suggests the reforms could provide a £10bn boost to the economy through improved wellbeing and productivity, lowering net costs to between £300m and £1.4bn.

The cost range depends on the threshold of hours the policy applies to; the government prefers between 8 and 20 hours a week, but unions have called for up to 48 hours. While the £10bn benefit figure is not fully monetised and business groups dispute the cost-benefit balance, the analysis indicates that when considering broader welfare improvements and productivity gains, the net cost could be significantly lower.
