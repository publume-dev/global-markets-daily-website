- UK 30-year gilt yields rose to their highest level since 1998 on Tuesday, ahead of the October Budget.
- The yield on the benchmark 10-year gilt rose to 5.22% on Tuesday, its highest rate since June 2008.
- Higher borrowing costs reduce headroom against fiscal rules, limiting Chancellor's ability to spend.
- Analysts believe mortgage rates on new fixed deals could rise as lender funding costs increase.

The rise in yields reflects market concerns about fiscal sustainability. Higher borrowing costs force the government to allocate more to interest payments, restricting fiscal space. Analysts suggest the chancellor may need to raise taxes or cut spending to meet fiscal rules, but this is not certain.

If mortgage rates rise, homeowners and prospective buyers could face higher costs, potentially dampening consumer spending and housing market activity.

Markets will watch for the Chancellor's specific measures in the October Budget and whether yields remain elevated.
