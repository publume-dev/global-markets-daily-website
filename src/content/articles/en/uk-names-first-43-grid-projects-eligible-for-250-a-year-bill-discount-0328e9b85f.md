- Eligible households live within 1,600ft (500m) of new or upgraded pylons and substations and will get £250 off electricity bills for up to 10 years.
- Roughly two-thirds of the 43 projects are in Scotland, with other upgrades north of London, East Anglia, the Midlands and North Wales.
- The discounts are funded by adding about 80p a year to all household energy bills.
- First payments are due early next year; eligible customers will be contacted by their supplier or Ofgem.
- An earlier assessment estimated 120,000-160,000 households could eventually receive discounts as more projects are rolled out.

UK ministers say the grid upgrades are necessary because capacity shortages sometimes force wind farms to be paid to switch off, and the work is intended to improve energy security and renewable-power distribution, according to the BBC. The report does not list all 43 project locations, so the full geographic spread and the exact number of households currently eligible cannot be calculated from it. The 120,000-160,000 estimate is described only as an assessment from earlier this year, without naming the author or methodology.
