- Treasury modelling indicates UK GDP growth could be as low as 0.3% in 2027 if Strait of Hormuz disruption persists until end-2026.
- The model assumes the strait is effectively closed for five months and no permanent US-Iran peace deal until the new year.
- Under this scenario, UK inflation would peak at 4.3% in early 2027, compared with the current 2.6%.
- The OBR forecast for 2027 GDP growth is 1.6%, compared with the Treasury's modelled 0.3%.

The analysis is based on Treasury modelling described as a 'reasonable worst-case scenario' rather than a central forecast, with sources remaining anonymous. The model shows that prolonged disruption in the Strait of Hormuz could reduce 2027 UK GDP growth to 0.3%, far below the OBR's 1.6% projection, while inflation could peak at 4.3%, up from 2.6%. This highlights the potential severity of geopolitical risks on the UK economy, though actual impact depends on the conflict's duration and global responses.

If this scenario materializes, the UK economy could face a combination of significantly weaker growth and higher inflation, affecting business investment and consumer spending. Furthermore, the gap with the OBR forecast may prompt fiscal policy adjustments to mitigate downside risks. However, given the model's worst-case nature, the actual degree of impact remains uncertain.
