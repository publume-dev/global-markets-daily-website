- The 30-year Treasury yield rose to approximately 5.27% on Friday.
- Earlier in the week, the Treasury announced it would buy back more debt to lower borrowing costs, causing yields to fall from 5.34% to 5.18%.
- US national debt surpassed $40 trillion, having more than doubled from just under $20 trillion in 2016.
- The dollar weakened, and gold climbed to a more than three-month high on Friday.

The Treasury's buyback announcement initially lowered yields, but the effect was short-lived, with yields rising back near earlier levels on Friday. This volatility suggests persistent market concerns about government debt levels. The dollar's weakness and gold's rise reflect investors seeking safe-haven assets amid bond market turbulence.

Higher borrowing costs could strain government interest payments and potentially feed into consumer and business loan rates. A weaker dollar could affect global trade, while rising gold prices suggest investors hedging against uncertainty.

Watch for further Treasury measures to manage borrowing costs and whether bond market volatility continues. Also, monitor the implications of rising debt levels on long-term fiscal health.
