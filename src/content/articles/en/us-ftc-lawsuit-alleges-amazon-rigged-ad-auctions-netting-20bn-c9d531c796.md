- The US Federal Trade Commission and 22 states filed a lawsuit alleging Amazon secretly overcharged more than a million advertisers by manipulating auctions used to set ad prices.
- The lawsuit claims the scheme has likely netted Amazon $20bn from advertising customers since 2019.
- The complaint accuses Amazon of charging advertisers their own winning bid close to 80% of the time in sponsored products auctions, contrary to expected second-price auction mechanics.
- Amazon shares fell 2.5% on Monday following the announcement.
- Amazon disputed the allegations, stating the FTC 'fundamentally misunderstands how advertisers operate' and that average winning bids on Sponsored Products fell 50% from 2019 to 2025, with about 92% of placed ads not given to the highest bidder.

If the FTC's allegations hold, Amazon's ad auction system may not operate as expected, potentially imposing higher costs on advertisers. Amazon's response offers a contrasting view, noting that winning bids have fallen and that most ads are not placed by the highest bidder, suggesting its system may not be designed to maximize revenue. Both the complaint and the response are unverified, and the legal outcome remains pending.
