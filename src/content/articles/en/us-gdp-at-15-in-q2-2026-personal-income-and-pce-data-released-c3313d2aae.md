- Real GDP increased at an annual rate of 1.5% in Q2 2026, unchanged from the advance estimate.
- The price index for gross domestic purchases increased 5.8% in Q2, revised up 0.1 percentage point.
- The PCE price index increased 5.3% in Q2, revised up 0.2 percentage point; core PCE (ex food and energy) increased 3.6%, revised up 0.2 percentage point.
- Real gross domestic income (GDI) increased 2.2% in Q2, compared with 1.2% in Q1.
- Corporate profits from current production increased $400.9 billion in Q2, compared with an increase of $74.4 billion in Q1.
- Personal income increased $115.1 billion (0.4% monthly rate) in July; PCE price index increased 0.2% from the preceding month and 3.7% from one year ago; core PCE increased 0.2% monthly and 3.3% annually.
- The personal saving rate was 3.0% in July.

Q2 GDP growth was unchanged from the advance estimate, but price indexes were revised up, indicating higher inflation pressures than initially reported. Corporate profits accelerated sharply from $74.4 billion in Q1 to $400.9 billion in Q2, suggesting stronger profitability. Although GDI growth was faster than in Q1, the gap between GDP and GDI persists. July PCE inflation at 3.7% year over year is below the Q2 annualized rate of 5.3%, but the monthly gain of 0.2% is moderate, hinting at a possible slowdown. The personal saving rate fell to 3.0%, which could affect future consumption.

The upward revisions to inflation and the strong corporate profit growth could influence the Federal Reserve's monetary policy path and market expectations for the economic outlook. Persistently high inflation might prompt policy tightening, while profit growth could support equity markets. However, the decline in the personal saving rate may weigh on future consumer spending.

Watch for subsequent economic data releases, such as personal consumption expenditures, employment reports, and Federal Reserve policy statements, to assess inflation trends and the sustainability of economic growth.
