- The June 2026 deficit was $73.3 billion, down $4.4 billion from a revised $77.6 billion in May; exports fell $2.9 billion to $314.7 billion and imports fell $7.3 billion to $388.0 billion.
- The goods deficit narrowed $3.9 billion to $102.1 billion, while the services surplus rose $0.5 billion to $28.8 billion.
- The three-month moving average deficit rose $5.6 billion to $68.5 billion for the three months ending in June.
- On a Census basis, the largest June goods-trade deficits were with Vietnam ($21.6 billion), Mexico ($20.3 billion), and China ($15.3 billion); the largest surpluses were with the Netherlands ($7.2 billion), South and Central America ($5.6 billion), and Hong Kong ($3.2 billion).

Through June, the year-to-date deficit was $189.3 billion lower than in the same 2025 period, a 33.8% decline; exports rose $198.3 billion (11.7%) while imports rose only $9.0 billion (0.4%). The monthly narrowing is arithmetically consistent with imports falling more than exports in June, but BEA does not provide causal analysis for the monthly or year-to-date moves. The data are seasonally adjusted but not price adjusted, so nominal changes may include price effects.
