- The US has struck a deal with Venezuela to control more than 65 billion barrels of its proven oil reserves, as announced by President Trump.
- Venezuela's interim President Delcy Rodríguez stated the energy agreement will last 25 years and targets increasing crude output to 1.5 million barrels per day, with an investment of over $100bn and more than $209bn in taxes for Venezuela.
- US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the deal a huge win, stating it will bring nearly $100bn in private investment and support thousands of high-paying jobs for Venezuela.
- A US official told CBS News that the US government will retain 55% control of a joint venture with an experienced private operator in Venezuela, which received a 100-year concession to operate in the oil fields.
- The Wall Street Journal reported that US energy firms Chevron and Halliburton were nearing deals to invest billions in overhauling Venezuela's oil field infrastructure.

The agreement grants the US significant strategic influence over Venezuela's energy sector, potentially reshaping global oil market dynamics. If implemented, increased Venezuelan output could affect global supply and prices. However, the official text of the agreement has not been published, and it remains unclear whether it faces legal or constitutional challenges in Venezuela. Analysts question the feasibility of accelerating investment given Venezuela's political uncertainty, weak power grid, and limited export capacity.
