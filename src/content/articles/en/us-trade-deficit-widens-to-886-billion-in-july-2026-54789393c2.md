- The U.S. goods and services deficit was $88.6 billion in July 2026, up $17.4 billion from $71.2 billion in June (revised).
- July exports decreased $6.6 billion to $310.7 billion, while imports increased $10.8 billion to $399.3 billion.
- The goods deficit increased $17.6 billion to $119.6 billion, while the services surplus increased $0.2 billion to $31.0 billion.
- Year-to-date through July, the deficit decreased $188.4 billion (29.6%) from the same period in 2025.
- The three-month moving average deficit increased $11.9 billion to $78.5 billion for the three months ending in July.

The July deficit widened sharply as exports declined and imports increased. Regionally, the goods deficit with Mexico increased $7.2 billion to $27.5 billion, driven by a $7.0 billion rise in imports. The U.S. trade balance with Switzerland shifted from a surplus of $2.9 billion in June to a deficit of $0.6 billion in July. The deficit with Canada narrowed by $3.7 billion to $3.2 billion. For the second quarter, the deficit with Canada increased $11.9 billion to $13.7 billion, while the surplus with South and Central America increased $6.2 billion to $21.7 billion.

The widening monthly deficit could weigh on U.S. GDP growth, as net exports subtract from output. However, the year-to-date deficit remains substantially lower than the same period in 2025, suggesting an improving annual trade picture.

The report does not explain underlying drivers of sharp monthly changes in categories such as computers and pharmaceuticals. Also, seasonal adjustment and revisions may affect comparability; figures are seasonally adjusted but not price adjusted.
