- Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh, in his first Jackson Hole speech, said the Fed will 'have work to do' if it lacks confidence that underlying inflation is moving 'clearly and at sufficient speed' toward its 2% target.
- Warsh said his remarks should not be treated as forward guidance and criticized the practice of signaling future rate decisions, saying it 'overstayed its welcome'.
- US consumer prices rose 3.4% in the year to July, above the Fed's 2% target; another closely watched inflation measure is running at 3.7%.
- The Fed left interest rates unchanged between 3.5% and 3.75% in July for the fifth consecutive meeting.
- Following Warsh's remarks, rate markets showed growing expectations of a rate rise in September, according to CME data.

Warsh's comments are interpreted as a signal that rates could be raised, but he stressed they should not be treated as forward guidance, creating ambiguity about actual policy intent. The increase in market expectations for a September rate hike reflects traders' interpretation of his speech.

Should the Fed raise rates in September, it would affect borrowing costs, influencing spending decisions by businesses and consumers. With inflation still above target and rates on hold, this policy stance may face adjustment pressure.

Watch for the Fed's decision at its September 15-16 meeting and subsequent inflation data to see if policy shifts.
