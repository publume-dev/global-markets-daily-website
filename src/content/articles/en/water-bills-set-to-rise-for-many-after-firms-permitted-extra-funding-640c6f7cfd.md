- Ofwat has provisionally approved an extra £3.4bn in customer bill increases for 13 water companies in England and Wales over the next few years.
- Customers of Severn Trent Water, Southern Water, Thames Water, Wessex Water, and South East Water will see additional bill rises in the next two years on top of increases announced in 2024.
- The other eight firms—Anglian Water, Dwr Cymru Welsh Water, Hafren Dyfrdwy, Northumbrian Water, South West Water, United Utilities, Yorkshire Water, and SES Water—will recover additional spending through customer bills after 2030.
- Ofwat approved funding for schemes including new data centres in Manchester, additional wastewater capacity in Newquay, and work by Wessex Water to tackle PFAS pollutants originally planned for 2030-35.
- The decision is provisional, with a public consultation and a final decision due in December.

The extra funding will directly impact customers of some water companies with additional bill rises in the next two years, while others will see costs recovered after 2030. The approved sums are intended to support key infrastructure projects such as new data centres and wastewater treatment upgrades, but they also add to consumer bills. Ofwat's decision balances the need for investment with customer interests, but the final outcome depends on the consultation.
